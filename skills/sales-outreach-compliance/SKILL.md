# Outreach compliance

This is practical guidance, not legal advice. For anything uncertain, tell the user to check with a lawyer.

## Always
- **Honour opt-outs immediately** across every channel and every bot. Keep a single `do-not-contact` list and check it before any send.
- **Identify yourself**: real name, company, and a way to contact you.
- **Easy opt-out** in every marketing email ("Reply 'no' and I won't email again" or an unsubscribe link).
- **Truthful content**: no fake "Re:" subjects, no fake urgency, no claims you can't prove.
- **Minimum data**: collect only business-relevant data; store source and date; delete on request.

## By region (check what applies to the user's market)
- **India**: Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act — lawful purpose, notice, consent where required, data-principal rights. TRAI commercial communication rules — don't send promotional calls/SMS to numbers on DND/NCPR; use registered headers/templates via DLT for SMS. WhatsApp: opt-in and WhatsApp Business policy.
- **EU/UK**: GDPR / UK GDPR and PECR — B2B email often on legitimate interest with clear opt-out; consumer marketing usually needs consent.
- **USA**: CAN-SPAM for email (opt-out, physical address, honest headers); TCPA for calls/texts (consent for automated calls/texts to mobiles).
- **Canada**: CASL — generally requires consent for commercial electronic messages.
- **Australia**: Spam Act — consent, identify sender, unsubscribe.

## Before a campaign, confirm
1. Where did the contact data come from? Is that source lawful?
2. Which region's rules apply to the recipients?
3. Is there an opt-out and is the do-not-contact list applied?
4. Are all claims in the copy supported by `sales-context.md`?

## Stop rules
If a recipient complains, asks how you got their data, or mentions legal action: stop contacting, answer honestly about the data source if known, and escalate to the user (`sales-human-handoff`).
