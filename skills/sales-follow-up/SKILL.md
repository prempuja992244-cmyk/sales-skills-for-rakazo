# Follow-up discipline

## Find what is due
From the CRM/sheet, list leads where: last contact > planned gap, meeting happened without a next step, proposal sent > 3 days ago, or deal untouched > 14 days. Sort by deal value and lead score.

## Rules for every follow-up
- Add new value each time: an insight, proof, answer to a likely question, or a simpler next step.
- Reference the last interaction specifically ("on Tuesday you mentioned the Diwali rush…").
- One clear ask. Make it easy to say yes or no.
- Never guilt-trip ("I've emailed you 5 times…").

## Templates by situation
**No reply to first email** (day 3–4, same thread)
> "{first_name}, one more thought — {new proof or angle}. Is {problem} on your list this quarter?"

**After a meeting** (within 24 hours)
> Summary of what they said (their words), agreed next steps with dates, owners, and the one thing you need from them.

**After a proposal** (day 3)
> Ask which part they want to discuss, offer a 10-minute walkthrough, and restate the key outcome.

**Ghosted after interest** (day 10+)
> "Hi {first_name}, I don't want to keep nudging if priorities changed. Should I (a) check back next quarter, (b) send a short summary for your team, or (c) close this out?"

**Break-up** (last touch)
> Polite close, leave the door open, no pressure.

## Pace
2–4 days apart early, then 1–2 weeks. Stop after the break-up message unless they re-engage. Move to nurture (`sales-lead-scoring` grade Nurture).

## Logging
Record every follow-up and the next follow-up date in the CRM. Drafts go to the user for approval before sending.
