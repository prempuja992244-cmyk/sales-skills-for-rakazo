# Objection handling

## Method: L-A-E-R-A
1. **Listen** fully; don't interrupt or rush to defend.
2. **Acknowledge**: show you understood ("Makes sense — budgets are tight before year-end.").
3. **Explore**: ask one question to find the real concern ("When you say expensive, compared to what?").
4. **Respond**: address the real concern with proof, reframing, or options.
5. **Ask**: check it's resolved and move to a next step.

## Common objections
**"Too expensive"** → Compare to cost of the problem (time, lost sales, errors). Offer a smaller start, phased plan, or payment terms if allowed. Never discount on the first push.

**"No budget right now"** → Ask when budgets are set and who owns them. Offer to help build the business case. Set a follow-up for that date.

**"We already use X"** → "What do you like about it? What would you improve?" Position on a gap, not on attacking X (`sales-battlecard`).

**"Send me some information"** → "Happy to. So I send the right thing, what's most important — A or B?" Then book a short review.

**"I need to think about it"** → "Of course. Usually that means price, timing or fit — which one is it for you?"

**"Need my boss's approval"** → Offer a one-page summary for the boss and a joint call. Ask what the boss will care about.

**"We do it in-house"** → Explore the hidden cost of in-house (people time, errors, delays). Suggest a side-by-side test.

**"Bad experience before"** → Ask what went wrong; show how that risk is handled now (guarantee, pilot, references).

**"Not a priority"** → Ask what is. Connect to it or schedule a later check-in.

## Build an objection library
Log every objection heard with: exact words, deal, response used, outcome. Review monthly; update the best responses in `objections.md`.

## Rules
- Never make up proof, guarantees, or discounts not in `sales-context.md`.
- If the same objection repeats in 30%+ of deals, flag it to the user — it's a product, pricing or targeting issue, not a script issue.
