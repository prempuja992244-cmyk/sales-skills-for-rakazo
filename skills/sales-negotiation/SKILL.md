# Pricing and negotiation

## Prepare (before any price conversation)
- **Walk-away point**: lowest acceptable price/terms (from the user, never assumed).
- **Target**: what you want; **anchor**: where you open.
- **Trade list**: things you can give that cost you little (onboarding help, longer payment window, extra training, case-study participation) and things you want back (longer contract, upfront payment, faster signature, referral, testimonial).
- **Their alternatives**: what happens if they don't buy from you?

## Rules of the table
1. Never give without getting: "If we do X, can you do Y?"
2. Trade scope before price: reduce what's included instead of cutting the rate.
3. Discount small and slowly, and tie it to a reason (annual prepay, signature by date).
4. Silence is fine after stating a price. Don't fill it with discounts.
5. Put agreements in writing right after the call.

## Responses
- **"Can you do better on price?"** → "What were you hoping for, and what's driving that?" Then trade.
- **"Competitor is cheaper"** → Compare total value and risk, not line price. Ask what's included in their quote.
- **"Procurement needs 20% off"** → Offer options: smaller scope, longer term, or annual upfront for a smaller discount.
- **"We'll sign if you give X"** → Decide against walk-away point; if yes, confirm the signature date in the same message.

## Output
A one-page plan: walk-away, target, anchor, trades, likely asks with responses, and the message drafts. All discounts beyond limits in `sales-context.md` are marked `[NEEDS OWNER APPROVAL]` and escalated (`sales-human-handoff`).
